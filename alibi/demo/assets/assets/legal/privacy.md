# Privacy Policy

Effective 7 October 2026, last updated 22 October 2026

Alibi is a gym accountability app: you and a small squad of friends keep each other consistent with verified check-ins. This policy says what Alibi collects, why, who can see it, and how to get a copy of it or delete it.

Alibi is run by Neeraj Pratap Singh, an independent developer based in Bengaluru, India ("we", "us"). Questions and requests go to hello@alibi.fit.

## The short version

- We collect what the app needs to work: your email, your name and avatar, your check-ins with their photos, clips and location, and what you do in your squad.
- Your check-ins and proof are shown to your squad-mates only. Your name and points appear on the leaderboard.
- We don't sell your data, show ads, or use advertising or analytics trackers.
- Your location is read only while you are checking in. Nothing tracks you in the background.
- You can download your data or delete your account from the Profile tab at any time.

## What we collect

### Your account

- Your email address, used to sign you in with a one-time code.
- If you choose "Continue with Google": Google tells us your email address, your name and your Google account's ID, so we know it's you. We don't get your password, contacts or anything else from your Google account. The name is only offered as a suggestion for your display name.
- The display name you choose and, if you claim one, a handle.
- Your pixel avatar. It is made on your phone: the photo you start from is processed on the device, never uploaded and never kept. Only the finished avatar is saved.
- Up to four profile photos, if you add them.

### Your check-ins

- Gym check-ins: two photos taken with the in-app camera, the time each was taken, where the phone was (location, GPS accuracy and distance from the gym), and which gym.
- Home workouts: a short video clip and its length.
- Rest days and freeze requests, including any reason you write.
- Your streak, verified days, points, rank and badges.

### Your squad

- Which squad you are in, when you joined, and whether you lead it.
- Messages you send in the squad chat and in direct messages.
- Nudges and call-outs you send or receive.
- Your votes: validating or flagging a check-in, approving a freeze or a gym, and reactions.
- Gyms you add: the name you give and the location you added it from.
- People you block, and what you report (a photo, a check-in, a message or a profile), with the reason and any note you add. The person you report is never told who reported them.

### Your plan

If you use the training and meal plan, your answers: goal, where and how often you train, experience, food preferences and things you avoid, meals per day, your sex, age, height and weight, a goal weight if you set one, and whether you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have a medical condition, or have had an eating disorder. These are health details: they are used only to work out your calorie and protein targets and to keep the plan safe, and nobody else can see them. Also the weights you log, your answers at each two-week check-in (how training felt and how closely you followed the meals) and what the check-in changed, the training programs you build, the workouts you log (exercises, weights, reps and times), the food you log (what, how much, which meal and its calories and macros), foods and meals you save, calorie and macro targets you set yourself, the routines you save and the meal plan you choose.

### Error reports

If the app hits an error it didn't expect, it sends us a report so we can fix it: the error and where in the app's code it happened, the app version, your Android version and your phone model. Reports go to our own servers, not to a crash-reporting company. They are linked to your account, kept for 90 days, and deleted with your account.

### Feedback

If you send feedback from the Profile tab, we keep what you wrote, with the app version and your phone model, so we can find the problem. It is linked to your account and deleted with it.

### Settings and referrals

- Your country, so food lists and meal suggestions fit where you live.
- Notification choices and quiet hours, with your time zone.
- A notification address for each phone you sign in on, so alerts can reach it. It is removed when you sign out.
- Whether your profile is public, and whether you share your plan.
- If you joined through a friend's invite, the link between your account and theirs.

### What we don't collect

- No contacts, no advertising ID, no browsing history.
- No background location.
- No audio: the app does not use the microphone.
- No payment details. Alibi has no purchases today.

## Permissions on your phone

- Location: read when you start a gym check-in and when you take a check-in photo, to match the photo to a gym. If your squad verifies by squad-mates only, the location is still saved with each photo but is not checked against a gym.
- Camera: for check-in photos, workout clips, your avatar, and scanning a food's barcode.
- Photos: only when you pick a picture for your avatar, a profile photo, or upload a workout clip.
- Notifications: for reminders and alerts, if you turn them on.

You can change these in your phone's settings. Without location or camera, gym check-ins won't work; home workouts still will.

## Why we use it

- To run the service: sign you in, record check-ins, work out streaks and ranks, and deliver messages and nudges.
- To keep check-ins honest: the server checks each check-in's photos, timing and location, and your squad can validate or flag it.
- To keep people safe: acting on reports and blocks, and preventing abuse. A photo, clip or message that several people report is hidden until we review it, and we may remove it or close the account that posted it.
- To contact you about your account, such as sign-in codes.

We do not use your data for advertising, and we do not build profiles of you for anyone else.

## Who can see what

- Your squad-mates see your name, avatar, streak and stats, whether you trained today, your verified check-ins with their photo or clip, the gym, the time and where it was taken, your freeze requests, and the messages you send them. They see your plan only if you choose to share it.
- Everyone signed in to Alibi sees your display name, points and verified days on the global leaderboard, even if your profile is private.
- If you make your profile public, anyone with your link, including people not signed in, sees your profile page: name, handle, avatar, stats and your profile photos. Your profile is private unless you switch this on.
- Check-in photos and clips are never public. They are shown to your squad only.
- Chat is never public.

A squad's leader can open it to anyone. An open squad is listed, by its name, a line its leader writes, its size and how active it is, for people looking for one; anyone who joins becomes a squad-mate and sees what squad-mates see. Nobody's name or photos appear in that list. The leader can remove a member.

If you leave a squad, its members stop seeing your new check-ins. If someone joins your squad, they see the squad's recent feed from then on.

## Who we share it with

We don't sell or rent your data. We use a small number of service providers to run Alibi, and they process data on our behalf:

- Supabase: our database, file storage and sign-in system. Data is stored in Mumbai, India.
- Brevo: sends the email that carries your sign-in code. It receives your email address.
- Google Sign-In, only if you choose it: Google learns that you signed in to Alibi with that account.
- Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging): delivers notifications to your phone. It receives your phone's notification address and the text of the notification, such as "Asha nudged you". We use no other Firebase product, and no analytics.
- A gym directory, to find gyms near you: OpenStreetMap (through the public Overpass service) or Google Maps Platform (Places). It receives the location you are checking in from, without your name or email. Gym listings from OpenStreetMap are © OpenStreetMap contributors.

- Open Food Facts, an open food database: when you scan a barcode or search for a packaged food, our server asks it for that barcode or those words. It is not told who you are. Food data from Open Food Facts is available under the Open Database Licence.

Reading a barcode happens on your phone; the camera picture is not sent anywhere.

Face detection for the avatar runs on your phone with Google's ML Kit and sends nothing to Google.

We may disclose information if the law requires it, or to protect someone's safety or our legal rights.

## How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as you have an account. When you delete your account, your profile, check-ins, photos, clips, messages, votes and settings are deleted at once. Copies may remain in our providers' backups for up to 30 days before they are overwritten.

## Your choices and rights

- Get a copy: Profile, then "Download my data".
- Delete everything: Profile, then "Delete my account". This is permanent.
- Correct it: change your name, avatar, photos, plan and settings in the app.
- Control visibility: keep your profile private, and choose whether to share your plan.
- Withdraw permissions: in your phone's settings.

If you can't use the app, email hello@alibi.fit from the address on your account and we will act on your request. We aim to reply within 30 days.

Depending on where you live, the law may give you further rights, such as objecting to certain uses or complaining to a data protection authority. In India you may also raise a grievance with us at the address above, and nominate someone to act for you.

## Security

Connections to Alibi are encrypted. Access to data is restricted on the server so that each person can read only what the rules above allow, and sign-in codes expire after an hour. No system is perfectly secure; if a breach affects you, we will tell you.

## Age

Alibi is for people aged 18 and over. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you believe a minor has an account, email us and we will remove it.

## Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect. The date at the top shows the current version.
